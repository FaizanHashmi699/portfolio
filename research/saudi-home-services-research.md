# Saudi Arabia On-Demand Services Business — Research Report (Step 1)

**Prepared:** 24 August 2026
**Context:** Founder (technical) based in Pakistan. Co-founder/brother based in Saudi Arabia, non-technical, would run operations and manage labour on the ground. Near-zero starting capital. Goal: earn commission/margin from other people's labour, not from own physical work.
**Status:** Step 1 = research only. Step 2 (product + tech stack) is deliberately NOT decided here.

## 0. The single most important finding — read this first

The hard part of this business in Saudi Arabia is **not the app. It is the legal right to supply labour.**

Two facts, verified independently, prove it:

1. **Urban Company** — a multi-billion-dollar, publicly listed home-services company with world-class technology — entered Saudi Arabia directly, then **gave up 50% of its Saudi business** to partner with **SMASCO** (Saudi Manpower Solutions Company), forming a JV called **Yammak** on 1 Jan 2025. It then wound down its own Saudi subsidiary; its commercial registration was cancelled effective 24 May 2026. SMASCO's contribution to that JV was not technology — it was **manpower resources and licences**. SMASCO was the *first company to obtain a government licence to provide labour services in Saudi Arabia*.
2. **As of 26 January 2026**, MHRSD rules make manpower leasing, workforce supply and staff outsourcing **licence-only**. Buyers must verify the licence, and **payments to unlicensed providers are blocked**. Unauthorised recruitment services carry fines up to **SAR 250,000**.

**Implication:** the world's best product team concluded that in Saudi Arabia, the licensed-labour side is worth 50% of the company. Anyone entering with "we'll build a nicer app" is attacking the part of the problem that is *already solved and not scarce*. Your defensible entry is therefore **not to own labour** — it is to sit on top of already-licensed supply, or to sell software/demand to the licensed players.

A second, very serious point: **Tasattur (commercial cover-up)** — a non-Saudi running a business under a Saudi's name or licence — is illegal. Penalties include deportation and a **permanent re-entry ban** for the non-Saudi, and whistleblowers can be rewarded with **up to 30% of collected fines**. The "just use a local friend's CR" shortcut is the single fastest way to destroy your brother's life in Saudi Arabia. It is off the table in every plan below.

## 2. Is the market real? Yes — but it is not empty

- Structural drivers: rapid urbanisation, high AC dependence (climate → non-optional, recurring maintenance), Vision 2030 formalisation of previously informal labour, rising female workforce participation, and high smartphone/digital-payment penetration.

## 3. The legal gates — what you must clear before earning one riyal

These are the constraints that eliminate most naive plans. ❓ = confirm with a Saudi corporate lawyer before spending.

### 3.1 Labour supply is licence-only ✅ (the binding constraint)
- Since **26 Jan 2026**, manpower leasing / workforce supply / staff outsourcing is permitted **only through MHRSD-licensed establishments**.
- Buyers must verify the licence; **payments to unlicensed providers are blocked**; 60-day compliance windows applied.
- Requirements on both sides include valid CR + activity licensing, wage-protection compliance, establishment-file management, and **role-to-role consistency** (the worker must perform the same licensed profession — you cannot send a licensed cleaner to do electrical work).
- Expat labour outsourcing runs through the government **Ajeer** platform, under the **direct supervision of the service-providing establishment**.

**Translation:** you cannot recruit workers, hold them, and rent them out. That business requires a licence you will not get as a first-time, zero-capital founder. **Plan around it, not through it.**

### 3.2 Domestic workers (maids, household help) ✅
- All hiring/managing runs through **Musaned** (MHRSD platform).
- From **1 Jan 2026**, salaries **must** be paid through Musaned's electronic wage system — digital wallet or bank account per worker, paid at the end of each Hijri month, worker gets a Mada card.
- Hourly / weekly / monthly packages exist via Musaned; workers are entitled to a **paid weekly day off**; a Feb-2026 "Work Interruption" service handles no-shows.
- Employer violations: fines up to **SAR 20,000** and/or recruitment ban up to 3 years. Unlicensed recruitment of nationals: up to **SAR 200,000**; unauthorised recruitment services: up to **SAR 250,000**.

**Translation:** the maid/domestic-help category is the most regulated and most surveilled. **Do not start here.**

### 3.3 Tasattur (commercial cover-up) ✅ — the existential risk
Non-Saudi trading under a Saudi's name/licence → **deportation + permanent re-entry ban**, plus fines; whistleblowers get up to **30% of fines collected**. Any structure where your brother "really" runs a business registered to someone else is this offence. **Non-negotiable: whatever you build must be registered to a person or entity that genuinely owns and controls it.**

### 3.4 Can your brother legally own/run this? ❓ — **the #1 open question**
This depends entirely on his residency status, which I do not know:
- **If he is a Saudi national or has Premium Residency** → he can own a CR directly. Premium Residency costs **SAR 800,000 one-off** or **SAR 100,000/year** renewable ⚠️ — out of reach at zero capital, so this only matters if he already holds it.
- **If he is on a standard employer-sponsored work iqama** → he generally **cannot** be a business owner/partner without going through MISA foreign-investor registration and/or employer authorisation. ❓ One source suggests an expat can be named General Manager in the Articles of Association; another states expats can freelance legally only via Premium Residency, a registered business with a commercial licence, or **explicit written authorisation from their employer**. These are in tension and **must be resolved by a lawyer for his exact visa class.**
- The **Freelance Work Document (Wathiqat Al-Amal Al-Hurr)** is **reserved for Saudi nationals** ✅ — it is *not* a path for an expat brother. ⚠️ One source claims HRSD issues freelance permits to some iqama holders in certain professions; treat as unconfirmed.

### 3.5 Foreign ownership / MISA (your route, from Pakistan) ✅ with ⚠️ on numbers
- Under the **2025 Investment Law**, MISA "licensing" became **registration**; the old Saudi-sponsor requirement was abolished for most activities; **100% foreign ownership is now the default in services**.
- Eligible startups can receive an **Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) in as little as 24 hours** ⚠️.
- **MISA Entrepreneur Licence**: reportedly **zero minimum capital**, assessed on business model, founder credibility and Vision 2030 alignment rather than capital; no local shareholder needed ⚠️. **This is the most promising formal route for you two** — but it typically expects accelerator/VC backing, which you do not yet have.
- **⚠️ Capital figures are badly contested across sources: SAR 30 million (for 100% ownership), SAR 500,000 (most activities), and "no minimum" (Entrepreneur Licence) all appear.** My reading: large figures attach to **trading/retail** activities (one source: trading needs 4 international branches + SAR 10M), while **service** activities are far lighter. **❓ Verify directly with MISA before believing any number in this report.**
- Costs seen: application fee ~SAR 2,000; annual renewal **SAR 10,000–60,000** by licence type ⚠️; legal advisory SAR 15,000–75,000 ⚠️.

### 3.6 Cheap-entity reality check ✅
If the entity can be held by a Saudi national co-founder *who genuinely owns his share*:
- Sole establishment CR: **SAR 700–1,000** total including Chamber of Commerce.
- LLC via Ministry of Commerce: **SAR 1,200** + 15% VAT.
- **From 1 Jan 2026:** initial registration cut to **SAR 1,000** (capital ≤ SAR 500,000) and annual renewal cut to **SAR 800** for SMEs.
- Chamber of Commerce annual subscription: SAR 2,000–3,000 ⚠️.

**The gap between a Saudi-owned CR (~SAR 1,000) and a foreign-owned MISA entity (SAR 10,000–60,000/yr renewal alone) is the entire reason to think hard about structure in §7.**

### 3.7 E-commerce, tax, invoicing ✅
- Online businesses need: **CR + e-commerce activity + Maroof registration**.
- E-Commerce Law duties: disclose commercial identity, show **price including VAT**, publish a written returns policy, protect customer data. Penalties up to **SAR 1 million** plus suspension/blocking.
- **VAT 15%.** Mandatory registration above **SAR 375,000** annual revenue; optional between SAR 187,500–375,000. **Below SAR 375k you are not forced to register — a genuine early-stage advantage; price inclusive of VAT from day one anyway so you never have to raise prices later.**
- **ZATCA FATOORA e-invoicing** is required; Phase 2 requires **real-time invoice clearance**. Budget engineering time for this — it is not optional and it is not trivial.

### 3.8 Saudization / Nitaqat ✅
- Requirement is a **percentage** (Saudis ÷ total workforce), varying by sector, activity code and size — not a fixed headcount.
- From **15 April 2026**, only Saudis with **Qiwa-documented contracts** count.
- Sector examples: engineering 30% (5+ engineers), accounting 40% → 70% by Oct 2028, marketing/sales 60%.
- ❓ You must check your exact activity code on the Qiwa Nitaqat calculator before choosing it.

**Strategic consequence:** every employee you add drags in Saudization, wage protection (WPS/Mudad), GOSI, Qiwa contracts and iqama costs. **A model with near-zero employees is not just cheaper — it is dramatically simpler to keep legal.** This points hard at §6 Model A and C.

### 3.9 Payments ✅
- **Moyasar**: SAMA-licensed, Saudi-built; requires **CR or valid freelance document**; supports Mada, Apple Pay, STC Pay; ~**2.2% + SAR 1**.
- **HyperPay**: requires CR; ~**2.75% + SAR 1**.
- **Tap Payments**: ~**2.9%–3.94% + SAR 1–2**; ⚠️ *may* not require CR (do not rely on this).
- ⚠️ **No source confirmed marketplace split-payment / payout-to-vendor support for any of these.** ❓ This is a critical technical question for Step 2: if you must collect money and then manually pay vendors, that is a compliance and cash-flow burden. Ask each gateway directly about "marketplace / split settlement / sub-merchant" support before choosing.

**Note:** Mada (debit) dominates Saudi payments and is *much* cheaper than credit cards. Cash-on-delivery remains common in services and quietly kills marketplace take-rates — because once the customer pays the worker in cash, your commission depends on honesty. **Design for prepaid/card-first.**

### 3.10 Getting the money to Pakistan ✅
- Register with **PSEB** → **0.25% final tax** on IT/IT-enabled export income under s.154A, extended to **30 June 2029**. Unregistered: **1%**.
- **≥80%** of foreign earnings must arrive through approved banking channels to qualify.
- Payoneer/Wise must be **linked to a Pakistani bank account** so remittances are formally credited.
- ⚠️ Under Budget 2026-27, Payoneer/Wise report transaction data directly to FBR. **Declare everything; the informal route is closed.**

**Key structuring insight:** if your Pakistani side is a **software/services company invoicing the Saudi entity**, that revenue is an **IT export** taxed at 0.25% — one of the most favourable arrangements available to you. This is a legitimate, standard structure, and it is far better than trying to extract profits informally.

## 5. Business models — scored honestly

### Model A — Booking/demand layer on top of already-licensed vendors ⭐ recommended v1
You never employ a worker. You aggregate **licensed** service companies, own the customer relationship, take a commission or a fixed per-job/lead fee.

- **Upside:** near-zero capital; no Saudization; no wage-protection exposure; no worker liability; fastest to launch; matches the "we don't provide our own energy" requirement exactly; the "licensed vendors" badge is already proven marketing (Dari uses it).
- **Downside:** thin take rate (see §6); **disintermediation** — customer and plumber exchange numbers on job #1 and cut you out on job #2; quality is not yours to control but reputation damage is; you are dependent on vendors who can leave.
- **Critical legal note ❓:** confirm with a lawyer that pure **marketing/booking intermediation** does *not* itself constitute "labour provision" under the Jan-2026 rules. The distinction is likely **who supervises the worker** — the licensed vendor must supervise, dispatch and employ; you must only market and route. Get this in writing. **This single question determines whether Model A is legal.**
- **Disintermediation defences that actually work:** prepaid packages/AMCs (the money is with you, not the visit), warranty that only survives via the platform, vendor-side value they'd lose (scheduling, invoicing, ZATCA e-invoices, ratings), and never launching in a category where one visit fully solves the problem.

### Model B — B2B service contracts / FM subcontracting
Your brother sells monthly cleaning/maintenance contracts to SMEs, mosques, clinics, gyms, small offices, building owners; delivery is subcontracted to licensed providers; you build the ops software.

- **Upside:** **contract revenue**, not per-job crumbs; one B2B client ≈ dozens of consumer bookings; FM companies **subcontract heavily** ✅; almost no consumer marketing spend; plays directly to a non-technical, relationship-driven operator's strengths — which is exactly your brother's profile.
- **Downside:** slow sales cycles; relationship- and trust-driven (an advantage only if your brother is genuinely good at this); payment terms of 30–90 days will strain zero working capital; you need to prove reliability before anyone signs.
- **Verdict:** **highest realistic profit per unit of effort for your specific team.** Strongly consider this as the *actual* business, with the app as its operating system rather than as a consumer product.

### Model C — Sell software to Saudi service companies (SaaS)
You build the booking/dispatch/invoicing/ZATCA-compliant system and sell it to the hundreds of existing cleaning/maintenance firms.

- **Upside:** you monetise **your** actual scarce skill (technical) rather than competing on labour you don't control; no labour law exposure at all; revenue is clean IT export → **0.25% tax in Pakistan**; can be sold from Pakistan with your brother doing local demos and support.
- **Downside:** SMEs in this sector are notoriously slow, price-sensitive software buyers; long sales cycles; ZATCA Phase-2 integration is real engineering work; you'd be competing with established FM software.
- **Verdict:** **the best fallback and an excellent second revenue line.** Also the most natural by-product of Model B — build it for yourself first, sell it second.

### Model D — Employ/supply your own labour ❌ rejected
Requires an MHRSD labour-provision licence, capital, Saudization, WPS, GOSI, accommodation, iqama costs, and carries the tasattur trap. **This is what SMASCO does, and it is why Urban Company paid 50% of its Saudi business to get access to it.** Not available to you at this stage. Revisit only after §7 Phase 3.

## 6. Unit economics — the numbers that decide everything

**Consumer one-off (the trap):**
- Job value SAR 99 · commission 20% → **SAR 19.80**
- Payment fee (2.2% + SAR 1 on SAR 99) → **–SAR 3.18**
- Net ≈ **SAR 16.62/job** before marketing, support, and your own time.
- If customer acquisition cost is even SAR 50, you need **3+ jobs from the same customer just to break even.** One-off bookings lose money. Always.

**Consumer recurring (viable):**
- AMC at SAR 650/year · 20% → **SAR 130/customer/year**, one acquisition cost, predictable.
- 500 AMC customers ≈ **SAR 65,000/year** gross margin. Modest but real, and it compounds.

**B2B contract (the real prize):**
- One SME cleaning contract at SAR 3,000–5,000/month ⚠️ (estimate — needs field validation) at a 15–25% margin → **SAR 450–1,250/month from a single client.**
- 10 such contracts ≈ **SAR 4,500–12,500/month recurring**, from ~10 relationships your brother personally manages. This is achievable by two people. 10,000 consumer bookings is not.

**Benchmark take rates ✅:** Urban Company's partner commission is ~**28%** (27.9%–28.3% by tier); other sources cite 8.5%–25%, most commonly 20–22%; general home-services range is 15–30%. **Plan for 15–20%** — you have less brand power than Urban Company, so you cannot charge more than they do.

**The honest conclusion from these numbers: the consumer app is a marketing surface. The B2B contract book is the business.**

## 8. The 90-day validation plan (cheapest-first, no big-startup spending)

**Rule for the whole plan: do not write a line of production code until Phase 1 is done.** You are technical, so the temptation to build first is severe — building first is how this fails.

### Phase 0 — Legal clearance (Week 1–2) · cost: one consultation
- [ ] Establish brother's **exact** residency/visa class and what commercial activity it permits (Q1).
- [ ] Paid consultation with a **Saudi corporate lawyer** on: (a) does booking intermediation trigger labour-provision licensing? (b) which entity structure is available given his status? (c) which activity code to register?
- [ ] Check the chosen activity code on the **Qiwa Nitaqat calculator**.
- [ ] Confirm real MISA capital requirements for a *services* activity directly with MISA (kill the SAR 30M / 500k confusion).
- **Gate: if Phase 0 says the model is unlicensable for you, switch to Model C (SaaS) — do not improvise.**

### Phase 1 — Demand proof, zero product (Week 2–6) · cost: <SAR 500
- [ ] Pick **one city** (Riyadh or Jeddah for volume; **Khobar/Dammam/Jubail** for less competition — note B8ak is strong in the Eastern Province) and **one vertical** (§4).
- [ ] Brother physically visits **20 licensed service companies**; records: their prices, spare capacity, whether they'd accept a 15–20% commission for booked work, and — crucially — whether they already turn work away.
- [ ] Brother pitches **20 SME/building-manager prospects** on a monthly contract, in person, with **no app** — just a WhatsApp number and a price list.
- [ ] **Target: 3 signed paying B2B contracts, or 20 paying consumer jobs, fulfilled manually via WhatsApp + a spreadsheet.**
- **Gate: no paying customers in 6 weeks with zero product = the problem is demand, and an app will not fix it.**

### Phase 2 — Formalise + minimum product (Week 6–10)
- [ ] Register the entity per Phase 0's advice; get CR, e-commerce activity, **Maroof**, bank account.
- [ ] Set up ZATCA-compliant invoicing.
- [ ] Open a payment gateway account (confirm split-settlement support).
- [ ] Build the **smallest possible** thing that removes your manual bottleneck — almost certainly an **admin/dispatch tool for your brother**, not a customer app.
- [ ] Standardise: price list, service checklist, vendor agreement, customer guarantee.

### Phase 3 — Repeatability (Week 10–13)
- [ ] Prove **retention**: % of customers who buy a second time / renew.
- [ ] Measure: cost per acquired customer, jobs per vendor per week, % jobs completed on time, complaint rate.
- [ ] Only after retention is proven, decide the consumer app / tech stack — **this is Step 2 of our plan, and it is deliberately not decided in this document.**

## 10. My recommendation in one paragraph

Do **not** build a general "book any service" consumer app — that is a head-on collision with Yammak (Urban Company's tech + SMASCO's labour) and B8ak (10 years, USD 2M raised, 8 cities), in a category where a SAR 99 job yields you under SAR 17. Instead: your brother sells **recurring monthly service contracts to SMEs and building managers in one city** (Model B), delivered entirely by **already-licensed vendors** so you never touch labour law, while you build the dispatch/invoicing software that runs it — and later sell that same software to other Saudi service companies as an IT export taxed at **0.25%** in Pakistan (Model C). It uses your brother's actual strength (relationships, presence, Arabic), your actual strength (software), requires almost no capital, avoids every fatal legal trap, and produces recurring revenue instead of one-off crumbs. **Validate it with a WhatsApp number and a spreadsheet before writing any code.**
