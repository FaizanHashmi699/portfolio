What to look at

Scroll down, then back up.

The navbar starts transparent over the hero, frosts to solid the moment you move, tucks away as you scroll down, and returns the instant you scroll up. Hover the nav items to see the pill follow. Narrow the window past 1024px for the drawer.

Transparent → frosted

Over the hero it's glass on the image. Past 8px of scroll it becomes a frosted bar with a hairline and a soft lift, so it stays readable over anything underneath.

Hides down, returns up

Only after 140px, so it never flickers at the top. Driven by transform, so the page never reflows and nothing jumps.

The pill follows

It rests under the current page and glides to whatever you hover or tab to, then settles back. With JavaScript off it simply doesn't appear, and the bold current-page colour still marks where you are.

Reduced motion respected

If the visitor has asked their system for less movement, the bar stops hiding altogether and every transition collapses. Stillness is the accessible default, not a degraded one.

Where the Islamic character lives

Geometry, not ornament.

Modern Islamic design is restraint. The identity comes from three deliberate choices — nothing is stuck on as decoration.

The khatim

The eight-pointed star is the atom of Islamic geometric ornament. It's the logo mark and the faint hero tile — structure, never wallpaper.

A bilingual lockup

منارة set in Amiri, a true naskh, sitting level with the Latin name. Most templates either omit Arabic or hide it in a footer.

Prayer before promotion

Prayer times sit beside the Donate button, not buried in a menu. It's the reason most people open a masjid site.

Honest by construction

No button here is decoration.

The WordPress build renders the Donate button only when a real destination is configured — no URL, no button. Same for Prayer Times. Nothing pretends to work.

Customiser-controlled

Label and URL for both actions live under Appearance → Customise → Manarat Navbar, with filters so Manarat Core can override them in code.

WordPress-native

wp_nav_menu, register_nav_menus, escaping on every output, translation-ready strings. No page builder, no framework.

Roughly 3KB of JavaScript

No jQuery, no animation library. One passive scroll listener behind requestAnimationFrame.
